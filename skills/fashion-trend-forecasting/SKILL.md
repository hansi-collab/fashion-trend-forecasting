# Fashion Trend Forecasting

Produce an original, evidence-led fashion forecast with the depth and practical design detail of a professional forecasting service. Use WGSN's publicly documented research logic: macro change → consumer need → market signals → product and colour direction → validation. This is independent research; do not present it as an official WGSN report, subscription extract, proprietary model or equivalent predictive accuracy.

## Scope and defaults

- Apply automatically to fashion trend, seasonal, colour/color palette, fabric, material, silhouette, print and key-item forecasting questions. In an established fashion conversation, a bare request such as “give me trends” uses this skill. Unrelated business, finance, software or statistical trends do not.
- Carry forward the user's current brief. Default to international womenswear when category and market are absent; the intended readers are fashion designers and brand owners. State a provisional customer/price position only when it affects recommendations. Do not infer target geography from the user's location.
- Resolve “next year” from the current user-facing date. Name the retail season and distinguish issue date, source publication date, observation period and forecast horizon. If no season is specified, cover the upcoming year with separate S/S and A/W implications rather than silently making a whole-year claim from one season.
- “Global” means a broad research scope, with regional climate, price and adoption differences retained. Ask only for a missing decision that materially changes the work; otherwise state a reversible assumption and proceed.
- Give the full forecast by default. For a quick answer or a specific colour/material question, use the relevant parts of the same method without forcing unrelated sections. A request for content uses the forecast as input; the existing fashion/content workflow owns scripts and campaigns.

## Research and synthesis

1. Browse afresh for current or future-facing claims. Prefer original collection notes/lookbooks, textile and trade-show material, official forecasting releases, actual platform/search data, retailer observations and primary consumer research. Read important sources beyond snippets where accessible. For supplied-source-only analysis, stay within that scope and identify its limits.
2. Use the relevant STEPIC lenses—Society, Technology, Environment, Politics, Industry and Creativity—to explain why demand could change. Do not fill all six with unsupported generalisations. Consult [method and source guide](references/method-and-sources.md) when researching the method or choosing sources.
3. Compare shows, social, shelf/retail, search and sentiment. Record what is supported and what is unavailable. Different brands repeating one aesthetic are design convergence, not independent evidence of search, retail sales and consumer demand. Syndicated coverage of one release is one underlying source.
4. Explain the trend's likely development: early signal, growing adoption, established direction or decline only where dated evidence supports that stage. Consider seasonality, geography, price tier, contrary evidence and the lead time to retail. A previously shown collection can support a near-term forecast; it cannot prove a prediction made before that show.
5. Develop original trend stories and translate each into garments, materials, palettes and decisions. Keep four things distinct: observed evidence, another forecaster's published prediction, our interpretation, and our proposed product specification. Put source links beside consequential claims.

Evidence boundaries: listings show availability, not sales; social visibility is not purchase demand; search indices are not absolute volume; a stockout can reflect supply. Do not invent data, numerical confidence, growth, sales, allocation ratios, material performance or colour-standard matches. WGSN's self-reported accuracy figures are not independently verified probabilities for our forecast. If browsing or relevant evidence is unavailable, deliver a labelled preliminary scenario with specific validation gaps, not a current verified forecast.

## Deliver the forecast

Use [the report contract](references/report-contract.md) for a full forecast or its relevant sections for a narrower request. Lead with the strongest recommendation and preserve the following useful depth:

- A dated brief and priority opportunities; macro drivers translated into consumer needs.
- Usually three distinct, original stories, each connecting evidence, anticipated timing and market fit to silhouette, key items, fit/proportion, materials/finishes, prints, trims, styling and commercial action.
- Visible colour palettes with names, screen HEX values, core/seasonal/accent roles, pairings and garment applications. Use an available visualisation or document tool when it makes the palette clearer; a labelled colour table is the fallback. A colour-only request still needs roles, pairings, application and evidence.
- Actual visual moodboards for every trend-story and season combination in a full forecast, generated and delivered automatically without a separate request. Follow [the moodboard contract](references/report-contract.md#visual-moodboards) for contents, generation, review and delivery. Three stories covering S/S and A/W require six boards; a single-season forecast covers only its requested season. Respect an explicit text-only or no-images request; quick or narrow answers do not automatically require the full moodboard set.
- Sampling and tech-pack implications, evidence strength with reasons, missing evidence, contradictory signals and a practical next review/test.

Keep verified official WGSN/Coloro/Pantone information separate from original palette proposals. Recheck names, codes, year and season at use time; never reuse a prior run's palette as current research. HEX is a screen reference, not a physical production standard or automatic Pantone/Coloro conversion. For manufacture, specify an agreed physical reference, substrate and lab-dip/material approval; do not imply testing has happened.

Deliver in chat unless the user requests a saved format. Honour the user's chosen document, deck, spreadsheet or visual format using the available tools. Image generation is part of a full forecast's moodboard deliverable. Do not require subscriptions, new software, publishing or scheduled monitoring just to produce a forecast. If image generation is unavailable or fails, complete the supported research and report the missing boards explicitly; prompts or colour tables alone do not complete the moodboard requirement.

## Before finishing

Check that dates and seasons align, linked evidence supports the actual claim and market, official forecasts are attributed, original colours are labelled, and product suggestions remain proposals where untested. For a full forecast, verify moodboard coverage against the story/season matrix and inspect each actual image using the moodboard contract. State what the research establishes, what remains untested and the next useful decision. For later updates, retain earlier issue dates so new evidence cannot be mistaken for an earlier prediction.
