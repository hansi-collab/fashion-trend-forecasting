# Public method and research starting points

These links explain the workflow or help locate evidence. They are starting points, not permanent proof of current product capabilities or a stored seasonal forecast. Reopen relevant pages at use time. A blocked page may have usable official indexed text; identify that access limit and do not imply access to the full report. Prefer accessible original evidence rather than repeatedly retrying a blocked source.

## Public WGSN method

- [WGSN AI and data](https://www.wgsn.com/en/what-we-do/ai-data): macro research, qualitative/quantitative inputs, the five product-data feeds and expert review. Public counts and service details can change; omit unnecessary scale claims.
- [Five essential data sources for fashion forecasting](https://www.wgsn.com/en/blog/expert-insight-5-essential-data-sources-fashion-forecasting): shows, social, shelf, search and sentiment; source combinations and different lead times. Published 14 July 2022, so distinguish its historical examples from current service details.
- [WGSN Future Consumer 2026 public sample](https://mlp.wgsn.com/rs/669-IKC-742/images/WGSN_Future_Consumer_2026_EN.pdf?version=1): illustrates STEPIC and the observe → synthesise → forecast → focus → re-evaluate loop. It is a dated methodology example, not a forecast for another year.
- [Behind the scenes of WGSN x Coloro colour forecasting](https://www.wgsn.com/en/blog/behind-scenes-how-we-forecast-wgsn-x-coloro-colour-year-and-seasonal-key-colours): global workshop, colour selection and feasibility checks. Published 29 April 2025.
- [WGSN Coloro tools](https://www.wgsn.com/en/products/coloro): physical/digital standards and colour feasibility. A standard identifies colour; it does not itself predict demand or verify a specific production batch.
- [Coloro key colours](https://coloro.com/key-colors): verify the exact forecast season, year, names and codes. Do not assume the page still shows the season the user requested.

WGSN's model details are proprietary and its vendor accuracy claims are not a substitute for independently reproducible evaluation. Different public pages may describe different services, planning horizons and measures. Do not invent a universal formula or proprietary score.

## Build the evidence mix

| Evidence type | Prefer | What to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shows/design | Original brand show notes, lookbooks, fashion-week organisers, material fairs such as Première Vision | Exact season, brand/category, observation date; design signal rather than consumer sales |
| Social | Actual dated platform observations or documented platform analytics | Geography/cohort, period, sample and metric; visibility versus purchase |
| Retail/shelf | Actual retailer assortments and documented retail-performance datasets | Region, category, price, newness, markdown/stock changes; listing versus sell-through |
| Search | Actual Google Trends or another documented search dataset | Query, geography, period, seasonality and relative-index limitations |
| Sentiment | Primary consumer surveys, interviews, customer feedback or purchase-intent studies | Sample, field dates, population and question; stated preference versus behaviour |
| Macro change | Original institutional, academic, industry or cultural sources appropriate to the claim | Date, geographic scope and credible connection to the consumer need |

Search and retail tools may be unavailable. Report that condition plainly and produce the useful, supported portion. Do not assert independent demand validation because several industry publications repeat the same trend direction.

For collections, use the originating fashion house's collection page rather than an aggregator when possible. For materials, start with the trade show's own seasonal research, then confirm feasibility with the actual supplier or test evidence. For another forecasting service, attribute its prediction and assess its methodology; its forecast is not an observed future outcome.
