# Professional fashion forecast report contract

Use this for a full report; scale it to the user's brief. These are content requirements, not mandatory headings or a fixed page count. For a quick or narrow question, preserve the relevant evidence, applications and limits in a shorter answer.

## Brief and recommendation

State research/issue date, target retail year and season, category, customer, geography and any material price-position assumptions. Make the planning horizon clear. Identify the strongest opportunities, their evidence basis, and whether the recommendation is explore, sample/test, expand cautiously, maintain or watch. Do not present a commercial investment recommendation as validated solely by runway evidence.

For an unspecified annual request, distinguish S/S and A/W. Retain regional season/climate differences. A long-range scenario needs plausible drivers and explicit uncertainty; it cannot borrow near-term product-model accuracy.

## Drivers and trend stories

Translate relevant macro changes into specific consumer needs, then product implications. A cultural interpretation is a hypothesis unless supported by consumer research. Mood words alone are insufficient.

For each story, usually cover:

| Component | Useful detail |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Original story name and one-sentence design thesis |
| Consumer need | What it could help the intended customer express or do |
| Evidence | Observations, source dates, market and evidence type; separate published third-party forecasts |
| Adoption and timing | Supported lifecycle stage, retail window and regional/customer fit; mark unknowns |
| Shape and fit | Silhouette, volume, length, proportion and fit intent |
| Key items | A small prioritised capsule with specific garment applications |
| Fabric and surface | Candidate fibre/fabric families, drape, texture, finish and material combinations |
| Print and pattern | Motif, scale, placement, repeat and colour relationship where relevant |
| Details | Trims, fastenings, pockets, seams, embroidery or other construction choices |
| Styling | Pairings, layering and coordination that make the story usable |
| Commercial action | What to test or carry forward, why, cost/execution constraints and alternatives |

Do not fabricate fibre percentages, GSM, measurements, tolerances or performance claims. If a useful specification range is proposed, label it as a development starting point requiring supplier and sample confirmation. Do not equate a fibre or aesthetic with verified sustainability, cooling, durability or protection.

## Colour direction

Create a coordinated palette, usually five to seven colours per story, unless the user requests another scale. Give each colour a role and practical application; a collection of unrelated fashionable hues is not a palette strategy.

Include original colour name, HEX, core/seasonal/accent role, suggested garment or trim use and at least one coordinated pairing. Explain relevant seasonal or regional changes. Suggested assortment shares are optional planning assumptions, never measured demand or a universal formula.

Show colours visually when the available medium supports it. Keep names and codes readable without relying on colour alone. Original screen swatches must not be labelled as exact official colours. For published colour standards, use only verified codes and authorised representations; otherwise provide the code and explain that the physical swatch is the reference.

## Visual moodboards

Every full forecast includes one finished visual moodboard for each trend story in each season covered, unless the user explicitly requests text only or no images. Build the story/season matrix from the actual brief; do not omit A/W from an annual forecast or invent extra seasons for a single-season brief. A request to update an existing forecast may reuse unchanged, approved boards and revise only the affected ones.

Use the available built-in image-generation tool, following its image-generation skill when available. Generate original concept imagery from the completed forecast; do not substitute a prompt list, a palette-only diagram or a promise to make boards later. Do not ask whether the user wants the moodboards. If generation is unavailable or fails, deliver the supported report, identify the exact missing story/season boards and leave their completion explicitly pending. Do not silently switch to a paid API or install software as a fallback.

Match the established editorial format: landscape 3:2, a light neutral paper background, legible restrained typography and a coherent grid across the set. On each board include:

- The exact story name, year and season, plus an original-concept label.
- The story's complete seasonal palette, normally five to seven swatches, with the same colour names and HEX labels as the written forecast.
- Four distinguishable fabric-texture close-ups labelled to match the proposed material direction.
- Two original print or pattern examples appropriate to that story and season.
- Three distinct key-garment examples or styled garment groups, showing full silhouettes and relevant design details. Product cutouts or invisible dress forms work well for clear comparison.

Preserve the forecast's approved colour roles, materials, prints, garments and seasonal differences. Use lighter constructions where the S/S brief calls for them and richer texture/layering where the A/W brief calls for them; honour regional adaptations. Keep garment examples original rather than copying a fashion house's exact designs. Label generated imagery as AI concept references, never documentary runway evidence or physical fabric samples. Swatch appearance is illustrative; printed HEX labels are references and physical colour/material approval remains necessary.

Inspect every generated image before accepting it: correct story and season, palette names and all HEX characters, fabric/print coverage, distinct garments, visible hems, readable text and no unrelated brands. Make a targeted correction for a material defect and re-inspect. A successful tool call or saved filename alone does not prove the board is correct.

Show all accepted boards inline with clear story/season labels. Save the individual image files and final generation prompts in the forecast's deliverable location; for a multi-board set also provide a downloadable ZIP containing the accepted boards and prompts. Preserve original tool outputs, exclude rejected candidates from the delivered set, and verify saved files and archive contents. In a requested document or deck, include the boards in that format and provide the individual assets when supported.

## Development and tech-pack handoff

Select the implications relevant to the proposed garment:

- Exact colour reference, substrate, lining/trim coordination, placement and approval status.
- Fabric construction, composition, weight, drape and finish still to be confirmed.
- Flat/design views, points of measure, fit intent, closure and pocket positions.
- Print/embroidery scale, placement, repeat and artwork requirements.
- Sample checks such as opacity, movement, seam behaviour, shrinkage, colourfastness, trim security or recovery, according to the design.

Distinguish a development recommendation from an approved specification or a passed test.

## Evidence and validation

For a substantial report, include a compact evidence ledger when it improves traceability:

| Claim/signal | Source and publication/observation date | Category/market | Evidence type | Interpretation and limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Source dates can be unknown; label that rather than inventing one. A collection season is not its publication date. Record collection observations as design evidence and forecasts as forecasts. Shared partners/press releases may not be independent corroboration.

Use qualitative evidence strength with a reason: for example, “design convergence across collections; search and sales evidence unavailable.” Do not turn an editorial score into a probability. A missing source type is a reported gap, not a reason to fabricate a claim or to withhold all useful design work.

Recommend a bounded next test: define market/customer, one or two candidate items, a success measure chosen before testing and a review date appropriate to retail/development lead times. Weekly capture and monthly review can be proposed, but do not claim a monitor is running or schedule one without a request.

If evaluating forecast performance, compare the report fixed at its issue date with later outcomes in the same market/category/season and a seasonally matched carry-forward baseline. Record misses as well as successes. Do not use later runway information to claim earlier forecasting success.
